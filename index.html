    Input format

    One wire per line: partId.PIN -> partId.PIN. That's the only thing that's required — parts are created automatically from the wires. Declaring parts is optional but gives nicer names and pin order.

    
    
    Built-in boards & driver modules
    
    
    Declare a part with one of these display names (e.g. part mcu "XIAO ESP32C3", no pins: line)
    and WireUp draws its full real pinout, with unused pins dimmed. Pins can be referenced by printed label
    or GPIO name (mcu.D4 and mcu.GPIO6 both work):
    
    
      
    
      Stepper drivers share the StepStick pin layout; clones vary on the small config-pin
    silkscreen, so check yours against the board in your hand.
      
    
      Tips
      
    
        
      
      • Power pins (VCC, 3V3, 5V, VIN…) are automatically drawn red, ground pins black. Other wires get distinct colors, or force one with [green] / [#ff8800].
        • 
      
      • Add a note in quotes at the end of a wire line — it shows in the wiring table and on hover.
        • 
      
      • The part with the most connections becomes the "hub" (drawn in the middle); peripherals go on whichever side their pins are.
        • 
      
      • Use left: / right: instead of pins: to force which side of a box the pins appear on, and board: pico inside a part block to force a board pinout regardless of the name.
